Pico dimmer node perfboard

The node on your perfboard: Hi-Link supply with varistor, fuse, diode and capacitor, the Pico mounted mirrored under the board, the Ra-02 beside it, a reset push switch and a status LED. Both channels of the AC dimmer are used: channel 1 dims the light bulb, channel 2 sets the fan speed. The load current never passes through the perfboard.

Check before you power it
  • The dimmer's VCC goes to 3.3 V (Pico pin 36), never to 5 V. The module pulls Z-C up to VCC, and the RP2040's pins are not 5 V tolerant.
  • J4 runs straight across from the right pin row: GP26 (pin 31), GP27 (pin 32), GP28 (pin 34), 3V3 (pin 36), GND (pin 38). Pins 33 (AGND), 35 (ADC_VREF) and 37 (3V3_EN) sit between them: leave those three unconnected. GND on 3V3_EN switches the 3.3 V supply off.
  • Confirm the pin map below against the labels printed on the back of the Pico before you solder J4.
  • Unidentified parts (yellow "?"): the 5-pin socket on the top edge, the blue part at the Hi-Link's AC end, the brown round part (drawn as F1) and the white JST. Tell me what they are and I'll label them.
  • If the varistor and fuse are plugged into 2.54 mm sockets that carry 230 V, L and N sit only 2.5 mm apart and the parts can work loose. Soldering them in is safer.
  • If the Pico is on the underside, put Kapton tape between its back and the solder-side wiring: its test pads are bare copper.

Mains wiring, light and fan

How it connects

230 V L ─ MCB ─┬─ Hi-Link via F1 ─ 5 V (on board)
230 V N ───────┤
               ├─ dimmer AC-L
               └─ dimmer AC-N ──────────────┐
                                            │
               dimmer L1 ─► light bulb ─────┤ (back to N)
               dimmer L2 ─► fan ────────────┘ (back to N)

   J4 (5 wires, low voltage) ─ dimmer VCC GND Z-C PWM1 PWM2

L1 and L2 are the two dimmed live outputs. Each load's other wire goes to neutral. The module takes its mains straight from the MCB, so the bulb and fan current never runs through the perfboard.

Running a fan on channel 2

  • Suits ordinary ceiling and table fans with a capacitor-run motor. It does not suit BLDC or remote-controlled fans, which have their own electronics.
  • Take the old wall regulator out of the fan circuit, or leave it at full speed. Two speed controls in series misbehave.
  • The fan's triac gate is held on for the rest of each half wave so it latches reliably with a motor load.
  • Speed 1–100 is spread over 35–100 % power so the lowest speed still turns. From standstill it starts at full power for 1.5 s, then slows to the speed asked for.
  • A little hum at low speed is normal with this kind of control. If speed 1 hums without turning, raise FAN_MIN_POWER.
  • Keep each load within the dimmer module's rating printed on it.
This board and the dimmer carry 230 V
  • Mount the perfboard and the dimmer module in one closed, non-conductive enclosure. The dimmer's triacs and heatsink are at mains potential.
  • F1 (T1A slow-blow) protects the Hi-Link. RV1 (10D561K) absorbs mains spikes.
  • USB and mains at the same time is fine electrically: D1 stops USB from feeding the 5 V rail, and the Hi-Link is isolated. Only connect a PC with mains on if everything is closed up; otherwise unplug mains first or use a USB isolator. Never touch the board or the dimmer while mains is connected.

Finish and test in this order

  1. Low-voltage wiring, mains unplugged: wire J4, J7, R1 and J6 as in the drawing. With the meter, check that 3.3 V, 5 V and GND are not shorted, and that nothing touches Pico pins 33, 35 and 37.
  2. On USB only: upload LoRa_pico_dimmer. The LED should double-blink (no mains at the dimmer yet), the master's scan should find the node, and the reset switch should restart it.
  3. Light first: unplug, connect the dimmer's AC-L / AC-N and the bulb on L1, close the enclosure and power up. The LED changes to one blink every 2 s. Try dim <id> 50, dim <id> next, dim <id> off.
  4. Then the fan: unplug, connect the fan on L2 (wall regulator removed or at full), power up and try fan <id> 100, then 60, 30 and 0. The fan slider on the website works too.
