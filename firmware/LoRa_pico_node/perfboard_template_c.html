Pico 2 node perfboard

One 15 × 9 cm perfboard carries the whole node: the HLK-10M05 mains supply, the Pico 2, the LoRa breakout, reset and status LED, plus headers for the 4-channel relay module and the fan's stepper driver. A marked zone on the left holds all the mains copper, at least 10 mm from everything else.

Board layout

Component side, with the solder-side wiring drawn through the board. Numbers along the edges are hole columns and rows. When you solder from underneath, the picture is mirrored left to right. Solid lines are bare tinned wire on the solder side. Dashed lines are insulated wire: the four SPI lines have to cross each other on the way to the far row of the LoRa socket.

Things that are easy to get wrong
  • Mains zone (red, columns 0–11): nothing except J0, F1, RV1 and the HLK's AC pins. Leave column 12 and the top half of column 13 empty: that strip is the isolation gap. On strip-board, cut the strips at the gap.
  • The HLK footprint is approximate. Lay your module on the board and mark its four pins before you solder anything. The AC pins must sit inside the red zone, and its +Vo/−Vo pins at the far end.
  • LoRa socket rows are drawn 8 holes apart. Plug your breakout into the two female headers before soldering them, and move the right-hand row if your breakout's rows are spaced differently. The antenna end points off the top edge of the board.
  • Rev C: the Pico stands upright with its USB end at the top edge. The micro-USB socket sits about 5 mm in from the board edge, so a cable plugs straight in from above the edge. Cut a slot in the enclosure wall there. Keep the area between the socket and the edge free of tall parts.
  • Pico pin 23 (GND, right row) and pin 8 (GND, left row) are left unconnected, on purpose. On row 8, J4's VCC pin sits level with Pico pin 8, and on row 14, J5's +5V pin sits level with GP10: there is no wire between them.
  • The GND bus (row 3) passes under the Pico between its two GND pins 3 and 38. That is fine on the solder side, as long as no other wire shares row 3.
  • D1's stripe faces the Pico (VSYS side, left). The 5 V rail runs round the bottom and up the right edge to D1, so it never crosses the GND bus.

The relay module needs the 2026-11-03 firmware

The relay inputs are now driven open-drain: pulled to GND for ON, released for OFF. A standard 5 V relay module, with its JD-VCC jumper fitted, then switches fully off even though the Pico runs on 3.3 V. This is safe because the Pico 2's GPIOs are 5 V tolerant. Do not wire the ESP32 node this way: its pins are not 5 V tolerant.

Mains wiring and safety

How it connects

230 V L ─ MCB ─┬─ J0 L ─ F1 T1A ─ HLK-10M05 ─ 5 V (on board)
230 V N ───────┼─ J0 N ──────────┘
               │
               ├─ relay 1 COM ─ NO ─► load 1
               ├─ relay 2 COM ─ NO ─► load 2
               ├─ relay 3 COM ─ NO ─► load 3
               └─ relay 4 COM ─ NO ─► fan regulator ─► fan
                                       ▲
                    28BYJ-48 turns the knob = speed

Relay 4 switches the fan on and off. The stepper sets the speed by turning the regulator knob.

Current budget (5 V)

Pico 2≈ 30 mA
LoRa while sending (17 dBm)≈ 120 mA
4 relays all on≈ 290 mA
Stepper while moving (off when idle)≈ 250 mA
LED≈ 5 mA

The 3.3 V pin supplies about 155 mA (Pico + LoRa) of its 300 mA limit.

The board now carries 230 V
  • Mount it in a closed, non-conductive enclosure. Use a nylon screw in the top-left mounting hole, next to the mains zone.
  • Mains copper stays inside the red zone, with an empty gap of at least 10 mm to any low-voltage copper. Make mains joints with plenty of solder and no stray strands, and cover the underside of the zone with Kapton tape or conformal coating.
  • F1 (T1A slow-blow) protects the HLK, and RV1 (a 10D561K varistor) absorbs mains spikes. The relay loads are fed from the MCB, not through F1.
  • Relay contacts are rated 10 A 250 VAC; keep each load well below that, and switch the live wire.
  • USB and mains at the same time is fine electrically: D1 stops USB from feeding the 5 V rail, and the Pico's own diode stops the HLK from pushing 5 V back into the PC. Whichever supply is higher runs the Pico. The HLK is isolated, so the PC is not connected to mains.
  • Only connect a PC while mains is on if the board is in its closed enclosure, or the mains zone and relay terminals are covered. A build fault there would reach the PC and you. For bench work with the board open, unplug mains first, or use a USB isolator (ADuM4160 type). Never touch the board while J0 is connected.
  • USB alone powers the Pico and the LoRa module only. The relays and stepper need the HLK, because D1 blocks USB from the 5 V rail.

Build and test in this order

  1. Low-voltage side first, no HLK yet: solder D1, C1, the Pico and LoRa sockets, C3, C4, SW1, R1 and the J4–J7 headers. With the meter, check that 5 V, VSYS, 3.3 V and GND are not shorted to each other.
  2. On USB only: plug in the Pico and the LoRa breakout (antenna fitted) and power from USB. Pin 36 should read 3.3 V, the master should get ping replies, the LED should blink, and SW1 should restart the node.
  3. Relays and fan on a bench 5 V supply: feed 5 V into the C1 pads, then test relays 1–4 on and off, and the fan stepper with HAS_FAN 1. Use no mains loads yet.
  4. Mains section: unplug everything, then solder J0, F1, RV1 and the HLK. Check with the meter that L and N have no continuity to any low-voltage point.
  5. First mains power-up: remove the Pico and the LoRa breakout, close the enclosure and power through a fused lead. The 5 V rail should read 5.0 V ± 0.2.
  6. Full node: unplug mains, refit the Pico and the breakout, close the enclosure, power up and run a reliability test. Connect the loads last.
